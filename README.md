# Option Pricer: Black-Scholes, Binomial Tree and Monte Carlo, validated on SPX

An option pricing library (Black-Scholes-Merton with Greeks and an implied-volatility solver, a CRR binomial tree
for European and American exercise, and Monte Carlo with variance reduction and Monte Carlo Greeks), tested in two
stages: against closed-form results and its own internal consistency, then against real SPX option quotes from
OptionMetrics.

## Repository layout

```
pricer/
  blackscholes.py   BSM prices, five Greeks (raw and trader units), implied vol (Newton with bisection fallback,
                    plus a reliability flag), portfolio Greeks
  binomial.py       CRR tree, European or American exercise

  mc_greeks.py      Monte Carlo Greeks: pathwise (delta, vega) and likelihood ratio (delta, gamma)
tests/
  test_pricer.py    parity, Greeks vs finite differences, implied-vol round trip, tree convergence, American premium,
                    Monte Carlo standard errors, Monte Carlo Greeks, control-variate theory
analysis/
  01_optionmetrics_baseline.py      parity forward, implied vols and Greeks vs OptionMetrics, single vol vs quadratic smile
  02_optionmetrics_improvements.py  settlement-time T, SVI smile with arbitrage check, BS vs tree vs MC on market data
  03_yahoo_snapshot.py              first market attempt (Yahoo data) and the input problem it exposed
  04_model_consistency.py           BS vs tree vs MC on a historical-volatility grid
  05_mc_greeks.py                   Monte Carlo Greeks vs analytic Greeks
  06_variance_reduction.py          control variate and antithetic variates by moneyness
  common.py                         shared helpers

```

## Results

### 1. Implementation

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Put-call parity | holds to floating-point precision |
| Delta, gamma, vega, theta, rho | match finite differences of the price |
| Implied vol round trip (420 cases) | every result flagged reliable recovers the true vol; the rest are near-zero-vega options |
| Binomial tree vs Black-Scholes | median relative difference 0.02% on a 15,420-option grid |
| American options | zero early-exercise premium for calls without dividends, positive for puts |
| Monte Carlo vs Black-Scholes | z-scores across independent seeds have mean ~0 and sd ~1; SE halves when paths quadruple |

### 2. Monte Carlo Greeks (`05_mc_greeks.py`)

Six cases (calls and puts, in, at and out of the money, maturities from 22 days to one year), 200,000 paths each:

* Pathwise delta and vega and likelihood-ratio delta and gamma match the analytic Greeks: 20 of 24 estimates within
  2 standard errors, the largest deviation 2.44. Repeated over 150 seeds in the tests, the z-scores have mean ~0 and
  standard deviation ~1, so the estimators are unbiased and their standard errors honest.
* **Pathwise gamma is exactly 0 in every case** (true values 0.0006 to 0.029). The pathwise derivative of the
  payoff's in-the-money indicator is zero on every path; the true gamma comes from the kink at the strike, which the
  method cannot see. The likelihood-ratio method differentiates the density instead, and recovers gamma.
* The cost is noise: likelihood-ratio delta has a 1.6x to 3.3x larger standard error than pathwise delta.

### 3. Variance reduction (`06_variance_reduction.py`)

Control variate: the discounted terminal price, whose mean is known exactly. Calls with S = 100, sigma = 20%,
T = 1; variance measured across 1,000 independent runs.

| K | rho (payoff, control) | Theory 1/(1 - rho^2) | Measured, control variate | Measured, antithetic |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 70 (deep ITM) | 0.999 | 414x | 412x | 16.6x |
| 90 (ITM) | 0.971 | 17.2x | 17.5x | 4.0x |
| 100 (ATM) | 0.924 | 6.9x | 6.9x | 2.0x |
| 110 (OTM) | 0.850 | 3.6x | 3.6x | 1.4x |
| 130 (deep OTM) | 0.638 | 1.7x | 1.7x | 1.0x |

The measured reduction matches the formula to within 1%. It is largest deep in the money because that payoff is
almost linear in S_T (the option is nearly a forward). Combining antithetic variates with the control variate is
worse than the control variate alone (ATM: 3.6x vs 6.9x): once the control variate removes the linear part of the
payoff, what remains is roughly symmetric in z, so the z / -z pairs are positively correlated.

### 4. Market data: OptionMetrics SPX, 2024-08-29

Three AM-settled monthly expiries (22, 50 and 78 days), out-of-the-money options with K/F between 0.80 and 1.10
(805 options). The forward and discount factor are implied from put-call parity, and implied vols are solved from
bid/ask mids.

**Consistency with OptionMetrics.** The parity forward is within 0.01-0.07% of the forward from OptionMetrics' zero
curve and dividend yield. Using OptionMetrics' implied vols, delta matches within 0.008, and gamma and vega within
about 1% except for the shortest expiry, where the time-to-expiry convention matters (below).

**A constant volatility cannot fit the skew.** With the at-the-money vol at every strike, puts below K/F = 0.90 are
underpriced by 19.0 / 13.4 / 10.9 vol points (22 / 50 / 78 days). The single vol that minimizes total squared error
does not fix this.

**Time to expiry.** Quotes are end of day; AM-settled SPX settles at the next morning's 09:30 open on expiry day and
PM-settled SPXW at the 16:00 close. Measuring T to the settlement moment shrinks the gap between AM and PM implied
vols on the same expiry date from 0.21 to 0.13 points (22 days). The remainder reflects that the PM contract's
extra hours are a trading session, which carries more variance than clock time suggests.

**Smile fit on held-out strikes** (fit on even strikes, tested on odd strikes):

| | 22 days | 50 days | 78 days |
|---|---|---|---|
| IV RMSE, quadratic -> SVI | 1.32 -> 0.25 pts | 0.69 -> 0.10 pts | 0.33 -> 0.05 pts |
| Upside wing (K/F > 1.03), quadratic -> SVI | 2.94 -> 0.45 pts | 1.51 -> 0.16 pts | 0.70 -> 0.06 pts |
| Median absolute price error, SVI | 2.9% | 1.0% | 0.5% |
| Share of prices inside the bid-ask spread, SVI | 31% | 47% | 82% |

The SVI slices satisfy Durrleman's butterfly no-arbitrage condition on k from -0.5 to 0.3.

**Next day.** The 2024-08-29 SVI fits applied to 2024-08-30 quotes (SPX +1.01%) give IV RMSE of 0.85 / 0.72 /
0.61 points, against 11.4 / 7.9 / 6.4 for a single vol. The fits are about 0.6 points too high on average because
implied vol fell as the index rose ("spot up, vol down"), which a static smile cannot anticipate.

**Three pricing methods on market data.** With SVI vols, Black-Scholes, the European tree (500 steps) and Monte
Carlo (200,000 paths) give essentially the same errors against market mids (78 days: 0.45% / 0.46% / 0.57% median),
the tree agrees with Black-Scholes within 0.1%, and 94-95% of Monte Carlo z-scores are within 2. The fit to the
market is determined by the volatility input, not the pricing method.

### 5. First attempt: Yahoo data (`03_yahoo_snapshot.py`)

In the Yahoo snapshot, the spot price was the previous day's close, out of sync with the option quotes, so call and
put implied vols at the same strike disagreed by 3-5 points. Implying the forward from put-call parity removed the
inconsistency, and that became the standard approach for the OptionMetrics tests. Yahoo's own implied-vol column
contained placeholders and disagreed with mids, so implied vols are always solved from mids here.

## Notes on Monte Carlo standard errors

* With antithetic variates, `mc_euro_vectorized` computes the standard error from pair averages. Treating z and -z
  as independent draws understated the error by about 30% when combined with the control variate.
* Comparisons across many options use a different seed per option. Reusing one seed makes the sampling errors move
  together across strikes, so their z-scores are not independent (the saved model-grid prices in `04` appear to
  share a seed, which is why they look better than the expected 95%).

## Limitations and next steps

* Clock-time T treats overnight and trading hours alike; a variance-time clock would weight trading hours more.
* SVI is fitted one expiry at a time; SSVI across expiries would also enforce calendar no-arbitrage.
* The parity-implied financing rate (5.5-6.1%) is above the zero curve (about 5.2%); separating noise from a funding
  spread would need more dates.
* Greeks are checked for accuracy, not yet for hedging performance. A delta-hedging backtest over many days, comparing
  Black-Scholes delta with a skew-adjusted delta, is the natural next test.
